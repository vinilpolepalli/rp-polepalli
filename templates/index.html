Effects of Epigallocatechin Gallate (EGCG) and Piperine Compounds on the Regeneration of D. Tigrina

Vinil Polepalli, Mr. Craig Queenan

High Technology High School, Lincroft, NJ, USA

  • Paper
  • Poster
  • Lab Notebook

Background

This research project tested the potential of Epigallocatechin Gallate (EGCG) & Piperine compounds on the Regeneration of Dugesia Tigrina (Planaria) to see if they can be utilized as a potential cancer treatment together.

EGCG

  • Most abundant antioxidant and catechin in green tea.
  • Effectively inhibits tumor growth by targeting proteins such as vascular endothelial growth factor and epidermal growth factor receptor, which are crucial for angiogenesis and cell proliferation.
Piperine
  • An abundant alkaloid in black pepper.
  • Modulates the WNT B-catenin pathway implicated with key processes such gene transcription and cell regulation.
  • Enhances the bioavailability of co-administered compounds by inhibiting enzymes.
Dugesia Tigrina
  • Commonly Known as Planaria
  • Planaria is a flatworm that regenerates its entire body from just a tiny fragment.
  • Valuable model organism for studying tissue regeneration processes, the roles of stem cells, growth factors, and signaling pathways in the regeneration process.
  • Stem cells share signaling pathways used in cancer cells, like the Wnt, Hedgehog, and Notch pathways.

Rationale

Cancer treatments have been a major focus of the medical field for decades.

Current Methods of Treatment

  • Radiation
  • Chemotherapy
  • Hormonal Therapy
Drawbacks with Treatments
  • In 2019, the survival rate for individuals diagnosed with various cancers between 0 and < 10 years after diagnosis was approximately 55%.
  • 49% of cancer patients experienced financial hardship.
  • Enhance the bioavailability of co-administered compounds by inhibiting enzymes.
Problem Statement & Prospective Solution

Today, there is a need for a highly effective and affordable treatment. Compounds such as epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG) and piperine have shown promise as potential cancer treatments that fit these constraints.

Hypothesis

As a result of the medicinal properties of the two compounds, it was hypothesized that planaria treated with various concentrations of EGCG and Piperine over a 7-day period will regenerate at a slower rate compared to planaria grown in regular spring water.
